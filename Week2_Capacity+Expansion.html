01

What the model decides

A least-cost fleet is chosen to cover forecast demand across 8,760 one-hour intervals.

MINIMIZE Annual fixed costs+ Hourly operating costs+ Penalties for unmet demand→ LOWEST TOTAL COST
CAPMW

Capacity built by each technology. Fixed costs are annualized per MW of capacity.

GENMWh / interval

Electricity generated by each plant in each one-hour interval. Operating costs apply to each MWh.

NSEMWh / interval

Non-served energy: demand left unsupplied. The modeled penalty is $9,000 per MWh.

Balance, every hour

Generation + non-served energy = demand

Capacity, every plant and hour

Hourly average output (MW) cannot exceed installed capacity (MW). Hourly energy (MWh) equals average output (MW) multiplied by the one-hour interval.

Covering peaks. CT has the lowest fixed cost of these thermal options but the highest variable cost, suiting infrequent use. The notebook's solved case therefore favors CCGT for most energy and CT for peaks.

04

How the lesson extends

The decision framework can grow, but these next formulations are explanatory extensions in the notebook, not solved cases.

Described · not solved here

Wind & solar availability

Renewable output is limited by the weather-dependent availability in each hour and may be curtailed.

Maximum renewable output = installed capacity × hourly availability

The notebook shows example January and July availability plots, but no renewable expansion optimization result is saved.

Described · not solved here

Existing plants & retirements

Brownfield planning starts with today's fleet, chooses new capacity, and may retire existing capacity. Past construction spending is sunk; ongoing avoidable fixed costs matter.

Implemented · solved

Thermal-only greenfield case

The notebook's solved model builds from zero existing capacity and chooses among geothermal, coal, CCGT, and CT. The saved HiGHS status is optimal.

Keep in mind

The solved case assumes no existing generation capacity. It omits energy storage, ramp limits, network constraints, unit commitment, and operating reserves. Demand is treated as inelastic, with non-served energy represented by a penalty. This dashboard presents saved notebook information only; it does not solve new scenarios.
